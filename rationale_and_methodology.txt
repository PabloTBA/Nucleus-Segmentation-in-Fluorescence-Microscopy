High Throughput Screening (HTS) is the automated execution of thousands of biological experiments simultaneously. HTS generates so much imaging data that it is nearly impossible for humans to manually annotate them all. The purpose of this research experiment is to compare which ResNet backbone U-Net architecture is best for nucleus segmentation in fluorescence microscopy. This is because the nucleus serves as the primary anchor point for analyzing a cell. By segmenting each nucleus in the image data, we generate a biological coordinate system. This allows us to quantify cell counts and measure morphological changes, such as nucleus swelling or fragmentation, which indicate cell death. All this also allows us to extract localized fluorescence intensities of other targeted protein biomarkers to build phenotypic profiles.The architectures compared will be 8 heavyweight industrial backbones: ResNet-50 and 101, Wide-ResNet-50 and 101, ResNeXt-50 and 101, and their Squeeze-and-Excitation variants, SE-ResNeXt-50 and 101.The dataset that will be used for this research project is BBBC039, with the link to it being: https://bbbc.broadinstitute.org/BBBC039For the loss function, we will use a customized combination of Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) and Dice loss. As nuclei often occupy a small fraction of the total imaging area, standard pixel-wise accuracy is misleading. We counteract this class imbalance by minimizing the loss function below:$$L_{Total} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \left[y_i \log(p_i) + (1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)\right] + \left(1 - \frac{2 \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i y_i + \epsilon}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i + \sum_{i=1}^{N} y_i + \epsilon}\right)$$The BCE term penalizes pixel-level classification errors whilst the Dice term directly maximizes the spatial overlap between prediction and ground truth.To compare the performance of the models against each other, I will first evaluate them at two different stages, which are the semantic and instance segmentation, and then on biological benchmarks. Because the goal is biological morphometry, relying solely on pixel-level matching is a common trap; if two touching nuclei are merged into one blob, a pixel-level metric might still score 99% accuracy, but the biologist's cell count and area measurements will be ruined.Pixel Level/Semantic metrics:
(Where $A$ is the predicted mask and $B$ is the ground truth mask).Dice Coefficient (F1-Score): This is the industry standard for medical imaging. It is highly robust to class imbalance (where black background pixels outnumber white nucleus pixels). It calculates spatial overlap, where an output of $1.0$ is perfect overlap:$$Dice = \frac{2\vert{}A \cap B\vert{}}{\vert{}A\vert{} + \vert{}B\vert{}}$$Intersection over Union (IoU / Jaccard Index): While mathematically related to Dice, IoU is stricter. It penalizes false positives and false negatives more harshly. It is excellent for comparing the absolute precision of boundaries between the different ResNet backbones.$$IoU = \frac{\vert{}A \cap B\vert{}}{\vert{}A \cup B\vert{}}$$Object level/Instance metrics:
These metrics are applied after utilizing our Multi-Head boundary-subtraction pipeline. By predicting a dedicated cell boundary channel, we bypass traditional watershed algorithms, subtracting predicted boundaries from predicted interiors to isolate and assign a unique integer ID to every discrete cell core. They evaluate whether you actually detected the correct number of discrete cells without splitting or merging them.Mean Average Precision (mAP) across IoU Thresholds: This was the primary evaluation metric for the famous 2018 Kaggle Data Science Bowl for nucleus segmentation.
How it works: You first calculate the IoU of a single predicted nucleus against a ground-truth nucleus. If the IoU is above a strict threshold (e.g., $0.5$), it counts as a True Positive (a correct detection). Instead of picking one threshold, the model is evaluated at different thresholds from loose to strict (e.g., $0.50$ to $0.95$ in steps of $0.05$) and average the precision scores. If the post-processing pipeline artificially splits a nucleus in half, both halves will have an IoU below $0.5$ with the ground truth, instantly triggering a False Positive penalty.Finally, after this, the models will be put against each other on the actual deliverables a biologist cares about:Absolute Cell Count Error: How close is the total number of detected nuclei to the ground-truth count?Morphological Variance: This will be mathematically proven using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test. By extracting the area and eccentricity of every predicted cell via scikit-image, the KS-statistic will definitively measure how closely the model's geometric distributions match the human ground-truth annotations.Post-Evaluation Interpretability Analysis
This will also be done, as in the biomedical industry, "black box" neural networks are heavily criticized; biologists and regulators demand to know why a model made its predictions before trusting it with high-throughput screening data.
To prove exactly how the winning architecture defeated the others and ensure it learned true biology rather than spurious correlations, the following analyses will be conducted:Visualizing the "What" (Grad-CAM): Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping will be used to track the gradients flowing back through the network and generate a heat map over the original microscopy image. This proves the model isn't "cheating." If the model is accurately predicting the cell boundary, Grad-CAM will physically highlight a glowing ring around the nucleus rather than random background noise or sensor artifacts.Tracking the "Heavy Lifting" (Feature Map Extraction): PyTorch hooks will intercept the tensors flowing through the network to plot them as grayscale images. Pulling feature maps from three distinct locations will prove what each layer is doing:Early Layers (ResNet Stem): Visually proves these layers act as edge-detectors, highlighting the sharp contrast between the dark background and the glowing DAPI stain.The Bottleneck (End of Encoder): Demonstrates highly degraded spatial resolution, where channels represent deep, abstract semantic concepts (e.g., dense cell clusters).Skip Connections: Visualizing U-Net skip connections proves how high-resolution edges from early layers bypass the bottleneck to reconstruct the sharp 1-pixel boundaries needed for the Multi-Head output.The "Why" (Comparative Error Analysis): The winning model's failure modes will be directly compared against the losing models to highlight architectural nuances. For instance, sorting the test set by the lowest image-level Dice scores may reveal that a model failed on images with massive, overlapping cell clusters due to vanishing gradients, whereas another maintained spatial integrity. Active isolation of pathological edge cases—such as severely fragmented nuclei from toxic drug doses—will be used to prove the winning model's robustness for real-world HTS.
